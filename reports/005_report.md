# Report: compartment readout (experiment 005)

2026-11-06. Numbers come from `reports/005/tables.md` (generated by `experiments/005/run.py`;
rebuild with `--report-only`). Cells are mean [95% bootstrap CI]; 30 seeds per condition, paired by
seed. Plan: `experiments/005/PLAN.md`, committed before the run.

## Setup

001–004 pooled all 48 MBONs. Here the punishment compartments come from the connectome:
- **Plastic set P.** 15 MBONs that receive ≥ 20 synapses from any of the 8 right PPL1 DANs. Most of
  them get input from one KC class.
- **Learning.** Dopamine depresses only KC→MBON synapses onto P.
- **Primary readout.** The sum over P of each MBON's response divided by its naive mean (equal weight
  per MBON).
- **Secondary readout.** The ratio readout from 004, restricted to P.
- **Rule-6 control.** 15 MBONs drawn at random from all 48 (a size-matched random grouping).

## Answers to the pre-registered questions

**Q1. Does real wiring beat matched controls with the compartment readout? No.** Real − shuffle,
real grouping:

| | sparse M1 trials | sparse M2 AUC (σ=1) | literal M2 AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary readout | +93 [59, 127] | −0.124 [−0.159, −0.090] | −0.066 [−0.086, −0.047] |
| ratio readout | +41 [4, 86] | −0.032 [−0.042, −0.021] | −0.057 [−0.072, −0.042] |

- Real wiring is worse than the shuffle on learning speed and noise robustness, under both readouts
  and both sparsities. It is worse still compared with random wiring.
- With the primary readout it shows less interference (sparse −0.018 [−0.036, −0.0001]). As in 001,
  this goes with having learned less.
- With the ratio readout it shows more interference (+0.028 [0.014, 0.044]).

**Q2. Does the connectome-derived compartment grouping beat a size-matched random grouping (real
wiring)? No.**

| | sparse | literal |
|---|---|---|
| M2 (primary) | −0.006 [−0.029, 0.018] | +0.018 [−0.002, 0.037] |
| M2 (ratio) | +0.009 [0.002, 0.015], d_z 0.44 | not significant |
| M3 | not significant | not significant |

- M1 cannot separate them: with real wiring the primary readout **never reached AUC 0.9 within 400
  trials in any of the 60 runs**, so both groupings sit at the 410 cap.
- The single nominal ratio-readout hit on M2 is small and isolated.

**Q3. Does the readout artifact shrink? Yes.**
- The sparse real − shuffle M1 gap fell from 214 trials (001) to 93 [59, 127]. The CI lower bound is
  below the pre-registered 107.
- Part of the shrink is because the shuffle also got slower with this readout (317 vs 154 trials).

## What did not work as intended

- **The primary readout makes the task harder for every wiring.** Learning speed with it:

  | wiring | 001 (pooled readout) | 005 (primary readout) |
  |---|---|---|
  | random | 143 trials | 216 trials |
  | shuffle | 154 trials | 317 trials |
  | real | 368 trials | never reached in 400 |

- Giving every MBON equal weight lets small MBONs count as much as large ones. For example, MBON35
  has 6 KC inputs and MBON32 has 28, and their binary-code responses are noisy. The resulting floor
  limits M1 comparisons. The plan fixed this readout in advance, so it was not changed.

## Exploratory (not pre-registered)

- **With shuffled wiring, the real grouping does help.**
  - Sparse: M1 −81 trials [−115, −48], M2 +0.082 [0.050, 0.114].
  - Literal: M2 +0.058 [0.037, 0.079].
- **Likely reason: KC in-degree, not compartment identity.**
  - The connectome-derived set has a mean KC in-degree of 279. That is higher than 98.5% of random
    15-MBON groups (mean 157).
  - The degree-preserving shuffle keeps those in-degrees.
  - Random wiring evens out in-degrees, and there the grouping makes no difference.
- **Interpretation.** Picking high-input MBONs gives a cleaner readout. This is a degree effect, so it
  does not show that compartment structure itself matters.
- **What is still needed.** A random grouping matched for in-degree is the right rule-6 control for
  that claim.

## Overall, after 001–005

**Across five experiments the answer to the project's first question is unchanged: no evidence that
real FlyWire mushroom-body wiring helps dopamine-gated associative learning over matched fake
wiring, with random odours.**
- Readout choices change how large the deficit is: about 2.4× slower with the pooled readout (001),
  never reaching criterion in 400 trials with the compartment readout (005), and close to the
  controls with ratio readouts (004, 005).
- No readout choice reverses the direction.
- The connectome-derived punishment compartments did not beat a size-matched random grouping when
  the wiring was real.

## Next

1. **Degree-matched random grouping.** Settles whether "compartment" means anything beyond in-degree.
2. **Structured odours.** Real PN→KC is biased towards specific glomeruli (Zheng 2022), and random
   odours cannot test that. This is the most likely place for real wiring to show an advantage.
3. **A less noisy compartment readout.** For example, weight each MBON by its KC in-degree, or read
   per compartment and take a vote. Pre-register it as a new experiment, not a fix to 005.
