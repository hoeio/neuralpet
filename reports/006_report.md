# Report: real odours from DoOR 2.0 (experiment 006)

2026-11-06. Numbers come from `reports/006/tables.md` (generated by `experiments/006/run.py`;
rebuild with `--report-only`). Cells are mean [95% bootstrap CI]; 30 seeds per condition, paired by
seed. Plan: `experiments/006/PLAN.md`, committed before the run.

## Setup

- **Odours:** 60 real odorants from DoOR 2.0 (Münch & Galizia 2016), mapped onto 47 of our 55
  glomeruli and scaled to the same mean input as the random odours.
- **Odour statistics:** real odours are correlated (mean pairwise r = 0.30, vs 0.00 for random) and
  activate more input units (29% vs 20%).
- **Task:** 18 odours per set, otherwise as in 001.
- **Readouts:** primary is the ratio readout (004); the pooled readout is recorded on the same runs.

## Answers to the pre-registered questions

**Q1. With real odours, does real wiring beat the degree-preserving shuffle? No.** Real − shuffle,
ratio readout:

| | sparse | literal |
|---|---|---|
| M2 noise robustness | −0.007 [−0.017, 0.002] | −0.021 [−0.030, −0.013] |
| M1 trials | +25 [−3, 60] | (all censored) |
| M3 interference | −0.001 [−0.015, 0.013] | −0.004 [−0.014, 0.005] |

**Q2. Does real wiring do relatively better with real odours than with random odours? Yes on noise
robustness, small.** The interaction on M2 is [(real − shuffle | DoOR) − (real − shuffle | random)]:

| | M2 interaction |
|---|---|
| sparse | +0.015 [0.0005, 0.030], d_z 0.37 (just clears 0) |
| literal | +0.029 [0.013, 0.044], d_z 0.65 |

- With random odours, real wiring is clearly behind the shuffle on M2 (sparse −0.022, literal
  −0.050). With real odours, it is close to the shuffle (−0.007) or less far behind (−0.021).
- The M3 interaction goes the same way in literal mode (−0.022 [−0.039, −0.004]).
- The M1 interaction cannot be read: with DoOR odours M1 is censored in 21–25/30 (sparse) and 30/30
  (literal) seeds per wiring.

## Also found

**Degree structure helps with real odours; random wiring is now the worst.** With DoOR odours:
- real beats random wiring on M2: sparse +0.029 [0.012, 0.046];
- the shuffle (0.694) also beats random wiring (0.658);
- with random odours, random wiring was the best on M2.

So the correlated statistics of real odours favour the connectome's degree distribution. The shuffle
keeps that distribution and does as well as the real wiring or better. This points to degree (e.g.
which PNs have many KC partners) as what matters, not the specific partner choices.

**The ratio readout is all-or-nothing in time.** Learning plateaus after the first block of 18 trials
(reports/006/learning_curves.png). With random odours the plateau is near AUC 1. With DoOR odours it
is ~0.80 (sparse) and ~0.65–0.72 (literal). Correlated odours share KCs, so depressing CS+ odours
also depresses CS− odours, and more training does not separate them. With this readout, M1 measures
whether the plateau clears 0.9 rather than learning speed. The plateau height (≈ M2 at low noise) is
the meaningful number.

**The pooled readout (secondary) gives the same picture:**
- With random odours, real is slower (M1 330 vs 130 trials, sparse), as in 001.
- With DoOR odours, all three wirings are near the cap.

## What this means

- **This is the first sign that natural odour statistics matter to the comparison.** Real wiring's
  disadvantage against the shuffle shrinks or disappears with real odours, and connectome-like degree
  structure beats random wiring.
- **The specific real connectivity still adds nothing beyond its degree distribution.** Real never
  beat the shuffle, in any experiment so far (001–006).
- **Caveats.**
  - DoOR covers 47/55 glomeruli and no multiglomerular PNs. Missing data were set to 0.
  - The task with real odours is near its floor.
  - The sparse interaction only just clears 0.
  - There are many contrasts in total.
